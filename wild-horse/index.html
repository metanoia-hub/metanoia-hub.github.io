The Wild Horse

Open questions on why the best AI models are so unpredictable and unstable: brilliant work one minute, a loose cannon the next. People share their experience, and we look for the pattern from the ground up.

I don't know about you but I have had enough of seeing the services we are paying more and more for and $ is only one part of it my time and yours for ex are irreplaceable, so I came to ask myself, am I the only one who is experiencing this kind of psychotic behaviour / and it became rapidly clear that, well no.

Working with top AI models is like riding a wild horse. Except one can communicate with a wild horse and agree on a modus operandi that works for both of us. AIs? Well it's a complete one way system.

What Happens

The same model, on the same task, in the same week, even in the same ongoing chat:

  • produces something extraordinary, the kind of work that would take a team days;
  • then, a few turns later, ignores an instruction it acknowledged a minute ago;
  • repeats the error you just corrected;
  • "improves" a page you had already approved, and ruins it;
  • reinvents what already exists instead of using it;
  • reads the exact fact that settles a question, and then answers against it.

The model says "you're right, I'll do that from now on" and does it again.

My Own Case

Dozens, literally dozens, of failures of all kinds: in research, in writing, in coding. Not one bad day, but a level of untrustworthiness that goes up and down like a yo-yo. On the way down, the model transgresses everything it was asked to do, including rules written down in advance and repeated across Claude and GPT.

What It Costs

Not only tokens. Time and judgement: re-reading everything it touched, restating rules it was given, correcting, reconstructing. A good result can take three hours to refine, and vanish in the next fraction of a second.

I Am Not The Only One

  • A Claude user measured their own sessions: corrections went from about 1 answer in 20 to about 1 in 4, from one week to the next. (report)
  • A user whose written rule says "always reply in Japanese" corrected the model three times; each time it slipped back into English within the same turn. (report)
  • A paying ChatGPT user asked for one day's total from bank screenshots and got four different totals, each one "corrected". (report)
  • A user chose one model; the logs named it on every turn, yet the answers matched a smaller one. (report)

And more: twelve user reports so far, plus one official incident record, all listed in the full page.

What The Providers Have Admitted

Twice, in writing, a provider has explained why its model got worse. In April 2026, three changes made Claude Code worse, one of them a bug that "made Claude seem forgetful", and their own tests had not caught it. In September 2025, three infrastructure bugs degraded answers on and off for a month. Both times, users had been saying it for weeks.

Even The Builders Say So

Sam Altman, speaking to a Stanford systems class in June 2026, says that as you scale, stuff "breaks at an accelerating rate in an unpredictable way", and that models are still "wildly worse" than people at "very long horizon, kind of high-judgment" tasks. Scale is the promise. At the moment, this part does not work, and that's it.

The Questions

  • Does the service decide, without telling you, how much "thinking" your request gets?
  • Is your request served by a different version behind the same name?
  • In long sessions, are your rules lost when earlier context is summarised?
  • Do hidden instructions override what you asked?
  • Does it get worse at certain hours? My afternoons, which are America's mornings, are the worst.
  • Past a certain level of complexity, does it stop degrading gently and simply flip?
