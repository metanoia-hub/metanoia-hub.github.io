Mtelos

A place for knowledge that already exists, held together and open to anyone, anywhere, in their own language.

This is my first draft. I'm giving the outcome I want, and trying to explain it in words you understand. I'm very aware it needs to be expressed better and defined better. If we can't describe what we want to do, nobody can help us. So that is the first help I'm asking for.

Why Mtelos: The M Is The Manifesto

The m stands for the Manifesto. Telos is Greek, τέλος: the end a thing grows toward, not the end as a stop but as the reason. The telos of an acorn is the oak.

Today's language models have no telos of their own. They predict the next word, and their purpose is whatever their owner needs. Mtelos has the one the Manifesto sets out: bring back the knowledge that was lost or kept apart, protect the people who carry it, and put it into the hands of the communities that live by it.

Knowledge That Already Exists

Most of what we need is already known. Institutions have been gathering it for decades: on medicinal plants in India, on traditional practice in Africa, on soil, water, energy and building, in archives, universities, seed banks and villages all over the world. And not only institutions: groups of people who got together and managed to gather it themselves.

What used to work is being updated all the time, because the climate is being disturbed. So there is a need for a constant update: what is happening, what works, what doesn't, what solutions are being found, and which of them can be carried from one part of the world to another, or from one kind of circumstances to another.

Most of them do not have the means to put it where anyone on the planet can reach it, in their own language. So it stays on shelves and hard drives, in one language, in one place.

What Mtelos Is

Mtelos is a place for that knowledge. Their collections, alongside the Winds of Change work, held together, connected across fields, and open to anyone anywhere, in as many languages as we can add as we go.

You ask your own question. It answers from those collections and names the source of each part, so you can check it and you know whose work it is.

Who asks? Farmers, people who take care of health, people redesigning a way of life that is independent, meaningful and sensible.

It is the foundation's work, with protection for those who decide to put their database in, because it then reaches more farmers, more precursors, more people actually using it on the ground. Each collection stays credited to the people and institutions who built it. Nothing is mined, and nothing feeds anyone else's model. It runs on our own machines, so it stays out of reach of any platform.

Why Our Own, Not An Open Or Closed Model

With someone else's model, there is no telling when its parameters get skewed towards the latest doxa, and it ends up not answering the questions, because apparently it would be hyper dangerous to know how the mitochondria works. Mtelos answers from the collections, and nobody upstream can tilt it.

What Counts As Knowledge

We do not take so-called scientific papers, Wikipedia and the like as the standard. We take them as what is available, alongside what was knocked out because it was "unproven", dug into a grave, or removed from common knowledge.

Yes, some of it will be considered controversial. People use it, and their use shows whether it works. Or they try it, it doesn't work, they feed back, and then we know.

Who Decides, And What Happens After Me

This is built in from the start. Looking at the precursors, people who gave lifetimes and sometimes billions to similar work, the main reason it fell apart was that nobody settled what would happen when they died. That is exactly why I'm taking care of it now.

Who Pays, Who Doesn't

Access depends on how people are involved. Those who bring their knowledge in are not charged. Those who work on it are not charged. Those who use it normally contribute, because a thing like this costs money to run, every day, and that has to be paid somewhere.

But Mtelos is made specifically for people who have no means and no way to reach this kind of knowledge. They are not charged. Along with Mtelos, we plan to give them tablets and internet access, so they can use it even in remote places, and feed back what they experience on the ground. Their feedback goes back into Mtelos. No open or closed model can do that.

The paid layer also gives us a little bit of an idea of who comes in. If it is big models coming to pick what we're doing, we say: thank you very much, go home.

What We Are Looking To Create

Not a small model on a laptop, and not another GPT, which would be ridiculous. Something in between: our own small data centre, holding a truly worldwide collection of knowledge. Where exactly in between is part of what we need help to define.

We know the outcome we want. We have ideas about what the different pieces cost, but not the whole shopping list. I'm not an engineer: if you can help us work out what it really takes, from the outcome backwards, we'd welcome it. Funding is handled separately.

How We See It Starting

Fully aware the processes will be refined as we go along: readers read. Annotators add notes and sources. Institutions and co-researchers bring their collections. Each piece carries a version and a date, so anyone can see how current it is.

People around the world can access the knowledge even if they do not have the means to load it or see what others have to offer.

A Different Kind Of Model

Part of our research asks whether a model can work by resonance instead of statistics, the way a chord sounds out its notes, by ringing rather than searching. There is serious published work in this direction, in physics, neuroscience and computing, and it is one of the threads we follow.
